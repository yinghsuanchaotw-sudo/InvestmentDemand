EXPERIMENT 3

Two effects of depreciation

Reset the graph. Hold r at 6% and raise δ from 10% to 15%. Higher depreciation has two effects:

1. Lower desired future capital: A higher δ raises the marginal cost, r + δ. The firm chooses a lower Kₜ₊₁, which reduces investment.

2. Greater replacement needs: More current capital wears out, so less survives into next period. The firm needs more investment to reach any given future capital stock.

Since Iₜ = Kₜ₊₁ − (1 − δ)Kₜ, these effects work in opposite directions. In this example, which effect dominates, and what happens to investment?

Model, equations, and assumptions

Production: Yₜ = AₜKₜαLₜ1−α. Capital installed today produces next period.

MPKₜ₊₁ = αAₜ₊₁Kₜ₊₁α−1Lₜ₊₁1−α = r + δ

Kₜ₊₁ = [αAₜ₊₁Lₜ₊₁1−α / (r + δ)]1/(1−α)

Iₜ = Kₜ₊₁ − (1 − δ)Kₜ

r = αAₜ₊₁[Iₜ + (1 − δ)Kₜ]α−1Lₜ₊₁1−α − δ

Fixed: α = 0.33; Kₜ = 100; Lₜ = Lₜ₊₁ = 100. Capital and investment are measured in illustrative units. Rates are per period. An interior optimum, fixed labor, no adjustment costs, and no financing constraints are assumed. Negative Iₜ represents net sales of existing capital (gross disinvestment); investment is not truncated at zero.

For depreciation: ∂Iₜ/∂δ = Kₜ − Kₜ₊₁/[(1 − α)(r + δ)]. The sign depends on parameters. Curves retain fixed axes so changes in scale do not masquerade as shifts.
